# Design: hunt, refocused on the document

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-08-05
Branch: `fix/paste-box-outgrows-the-dialog`
Status: APPROVED — with one question the owner never answered out loud, recorded
under Open Questions and to be settled before any code.
Mode: Builder

## Problem Statement

hunt's v1 was built, gated and merged without ever being used. On 2026-08-05 its
author used it for the first time and hit five defects in forty-five minutes —
a paste box that grew past the viewport, a tailor screen that scrolled off the
window in Safari, a blank PDF preview, no way to edit a saved posting, no way to
remove a skills group he had just added. All five are fixed. His verdict after
they were: *"it's still not something I'd use, it's far from a usable product."*

The defects were the symptom. Two things underneath them are the actual problem.

**First, the capabilities aren't the product.** The two things he ranked highest
already exist in the repo and neither is a place you can go. `parse-fidelity.ts`
renders a version to PDF, feeds it back through a deliberately naive ATS-grade
parser and diffs field-by-field — *"every field the parser lost is a field the
employer's ATS probably loses too"* — and it is the third of five rows behind a
Run button he never pressed. Job-board search has adapters for Greenhouse,
Lever, Ashby, Adzuna and JSearch and lives on a route he did not open. Both hang
off a pipeline CRM, and the CRM is what got the routes, the board, the table and
a dashboard.

**Second, the structured model owns the document and has no vocabulary for
presentation or for inclusion.** All three templates load `hyperref`; nothing
calls `\href`, so every link on the résumé is dead ink. `latex.ts` escapes `{`,
`}` and `\` — correctly, one stray `$` breaks the document — which also means no
word can be emphasised. The schema has no include flag, so omitting a bullet for
one posting deletes it for every posting. This is what he was reaching for with
*"i cant decide if certain things should perhaps be left out"* and *"certain
things i want bold vs i dont isnt perfect."*

**And the thing he cares most about is unmodelled.** He spent more words on
voice than on anything else: *"it feels written by me, not by AI… you can tell
something is AI with the vocabulary and grammar, certain punctuation, the way it
sounds non-human, punchy claims rather than natural flow."* On Firecrawl,
hunt proposed replacing his

> Bootstrapped and self-funded. Paid engagements building AI systems for
> businesses I already have direct access to: I find the problem myself, build
> it, and run it in production.

with

> Bootstrapped and self-funded. I find the problem, build the system, ship it to
> production, and own it after — no PM, no backlog, no handoff.

Triadic punch, em-dash pivot, claim density up, flow gone. The citation
validator approved it, because it checks provenance and nothing checks voice.

## What Makes This Cool

**Two honest counts and one earned one, where every competitor ships an invented
number.** Not "78% match" — *"12 of 15 fields survive a machine reading,"* with
the three that don't, named by path and clickable. *"The posting names 9 things;
your résumé shows 5,"* with the four listed. And, once there is history, the one
number nobody else can have because it isn't predicted: *"v3 — 11 sent, 4
interviews,"* which is a join over data hunt already stores.

The owner arrived at this independently and for a better reason than the repo
had. `COMPETITORS.md` refuses the score on principle. He refused it on utility:
*"most people prefer a score but for me and my goals it's not about that, so I
can focus on what's real."* A number you can act on in the next ten seconds
beats one you can only feel bad about.

## Constraints

- **hunt is a tool for its author, published openly.** Success is that he reaches
  for it during his own job search. Adoption is not a goal, so choices that cost
  adoption and serve him are correct.
- Local-first, BYOK, no telemetry. Unchanged, and independently validated by
  strangers in the Firecrawl thread choosing self-hosted searxng and local
  crawlers over metered APIs for cost and query privacy.
- Everything already true of the codebase holds: `pnpm verify` stays green, one
  logical change per commit, the DB migrates itself, the Prisma client stays
  lazy behind its proxy.

## Premises

Agreed 2026-08-05, with premise 8 amended by the owner.

1. hunt is a tool for its author, published openly — not a product chasing users.
2. The document is the center; the pipeline is support. The unit of work is
   *this posting → this résumé and this letter*.
3. No invented numbers, but yes to counts — fields surviving a parse, posting
   terms present, outcomes of versions actually sent. Each shown with the list
   that makes it actionable. Never merged into one score, never predicted.
4. Voice is a quality bar, not a feature. It doesn't get sequenced; it gets
   enforced whenever generation ships.
5. The master document is a superset; a tailored version is a selection over it.
   Include/exclude is a primitive. Deleting in order to omit is a bug.
6. Discovery is back in scope, ranked second — reversing the earlier decision to
   demote sourcing. Outreach stays demoted. Interview prep and per-JD progress
   notes are later.
7. Most of what ranks highest is built and buried. The work is promotion and
   surfacing, plus four missing primitives: include/exclude, real links, an
   emphasis vocabulary, and something that models how he writes.
8. **The three Application surfaces collapse to one. Outreach and sourcing keep
   their nav entries, marked "coming soon"** — a nav that shrinks reads as
   retreat; one that shows what's next does the work of a roadmap nobody has to
   open.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Promote what's already there
Zero new capability. Parse fidelity and keyword coverage run automatically and
live where the page count lives now. Three Application surfaces collapse to one.
Outreach and sourcing become "coming soon."
**Effort** S–M · **Risk** Low
**Pros** Fastest route to actually using it. Directly tests the session's central
finding — that the top-ranked feature was built and hidden. Small diff, nothing
to un-build if wrong.
**Cons** Doesn't touch voice, include/exclude or emphasis.
**Reuses** `parse-fidelity.ts`, keyword coverage, the live page-count component,
`PipelineTable` / `PipelineBoard`.

### Approach B: The document becomes the product
One workbench per application — master, selection, live PDF, live counts, letter
— plus include/exclude, real `\href`, an emphasis vocabulary, and a voice check
that runs after generation the way the citation validator does, scored against a
corpus of the author's own writing.
**Effort** L–XL · **Risk** Med
**Pros** Answers every complaint. Makes voice enforceable by code, reusing the
one pattern hunt already got right.
**Cons** Data-model change. Voice enforcement is genuinely unsolved; everything
else in it is known work.

### Approach C: A résumé compiler with constraints
The master document is source. Each application *compiles* a variant under
constraints: at most two pages, no field dropped by the parser, cover these
posting terms, sound like me. hunt proposes the diff that satisfies them — mostly
by cutting and reordering rather than rewriting — and the human approves.
**Effort** L · **Risk** Med–High
**Pros** One mental model absorbing selection, page count, fidelity, coverage and
voice. Makes cutting first-class, which is what tailoring actually is and what
today's version cannot do at all.
**Cons** Needs include/exclude first. Constraint UI is easy to make feel
mechanical.
**Reuses** the page-count floor logic, `applyChangesWithReport`, the checks.

## The verdict that reshaped this doc

Written after the approaches above, and it overrules the first version of the
recommendation. Given five real postings, the author's summary was:

> *"Right now I gave you 5 jobs, and I can't use this for any of those 5. The
> résumé I get out vs the one I uploaded, I'd rather go with just my résumé."*

**The output is worse than the input.** That is a value failure, not a usability
one, and Approach A as scoped does not touch it — counts and links do not make a
tailored résumé worth sending. It also breaks the exit gate: "apply to one real
job end to end" can be passed by ignoring the tailoring and sending the base.

### Tailoring selects; it does not rewrite

The fix follows from the failure. hunt already knows which of your lines matter
for a posting — keyword coverage and the fit rating compute exactly that. Today
it spends that knowledge rewriting your sentences into the cadence you object to
and then refusing half of them for want of a citation. Instead it should propose
**which of your existing bullets to include, which to drop, and in what order**,
using your text verbatim.

- The output is your own words, so it cannot read as AI. The voice problem is not
  solved, it is **sidestepped** — which is why voice can stay unestimated without
  blocking a usable product.
- Nothing is generated, so nothing can be fabricated, and the refusal path stops
  being the dead end that `NOTEBOOK.md` was designed to fix.
- The result is never worse than the uploaded résumé, because it *is* the
  uploaded résumé, filtered and ordered for this posting.
- Rewriting becomes opt-in per line rather than the default to be fought.
- And hunt gains a move no competitor will make: *"your base résumé is already
  the right one to send for this posting."*

This promotes include/exclude from Approach C into the first release. Selection
is the product; rewriting is an accessory.

## Recommended Approach

**A′ = A plus selection-first tailoring, then the rest of C, with B's voice check
as the quality bar if and when rewriting is turned back on.**

A is days, and it settles the question this session raised: was hunt's problem
ever missing capability, or only ever placement? If landing on a screen that
reads *"12 of 15 fields survive a machine reading"* makes him want to keep
going, C is the real shape. If it doesn't, that was learned cheaply and B and C
get rethought before a week is spent.

## Success Criteria

**The gate for A is not a feature list.** Feature-completeness and usability were
shown today to be unrelated — 847 unit tests, 21 e2e specs and 15 gates were
green while the flagship screen was unusable in the author's browser. So:

> **Sameer applies to one real job, end to end, through hunt, without anyone
> fixing code mid-flight — and sends the résumé hunt produced, because he
> prefers it to the one he uploaded.**

The second clause is the one that matters. The first can be passed by ignoring
the tailoring, which is exactly what happened across five postings today.

Supporting criteria, each checkable:

- For a posting where hunt proposes changes, the produced résumé contains no
  sentence the author did not write.
- For a posting where nothing should change, hunt says so, rather than
  manufacturing edits to look useful.

- Parse fidelity and keyword coverage are visible without pressing anything, and
  update when the document does.
- Every link in the rendered PDF is a real hyperlink, and parse fidelity stops
  reporting links as dropped.
- One surface over `Application`, not three.
- `pnpm verify` green throughout.
- **The e2e suite runs WebKit, not only Chromium.** `playwright.config.ts:20`
  declares one project, `chromium`. The tailor screen was unusable in Safari
  while all 21 specs passed, because no spec has ever run in the engine the
  author uses. Shipping A without closing this risks failing the gate the same
  way twice.

## Known Rough Edges After A

Stated up front so they are not discovered in week two:

- **Voice, if rewriting is turned on.** Under selection-first tailoring the
  default output is the author's own prose, so this only bites when a rewrite is
  explicitly requested per line. Still the only unsolved item, now optional
  rather than unavoidable.
- **Discovery is a search box, not a feed.** Ranked second; comes after.
- **The notebook is still not built**, so a genuinely missing fact — the summary
  the résumé does not have — still cannot be filled. Selection cannot invent what
  was never written; it only stops pretending otherwise.

## Open Questions

- **Unanswered, and it gates the plan: does A′ actually beat sending the base
  résumé?** The author was asked directly and moved on to something else. If
  selection-first tailoring still produces something he would not send, the
  recommendation is wrong and no amount of building fixes it. Settle this first
  next session — ideally against one of the five postings already loaded.
- **Are the Node bindings for `anydoc` / `pdf-inspector` native or WASM?** Both
  advertise WebAssembly as a *browser* target. hunt already runs two native
  modules under duress — `better-sqlite3` is version-pinned because two builds in
  one process segfault the server, and the Prisma client is lazy behind a proxy
  because module-scope construction segfaults `next build` workers. A third
  native addon is a real risk; a WASM path is not. Check before adopting.
- **How is voice enforced?** A post-generation check scored against a corpus of
  his own writing is the shape, following the citation-validator pattern. What
  the corpus is (résumé bullets, GitHub READMEs, past writing) and what the check
  measures are both unresolved. No estimate is given for this deliberately.
- **Does a version become a selection, or stay a fork?** C needs the answer; A
  does not. Migrating an existing version tree is the cost.
- **Does GitHub become a source?** He named it in the ideal — *"my projects, my
  github"* — as input both to what hunt knows about him and to voice.
- **What happens to `Outreach`, `Contact` and `SourcingRun` in the schema?** The
  nav says "coming soon"; the models stay. Nothing is deleted.

## Next Steps

1. **Include/exclude as a primitive.** A per-line flag on the version, not a
   deletion. The master stays whole; a tailored version is a selection over it.
   Everything else in this list depends on this existing.
2. **Import quality, because selection makes it a prerequisite.** Evaluate
   `firecrawl/anydoc` (MIT, Rust, PDF/DOCX/RTF/ODT → clean GFM through one
   internal model, no ML, no external services) as the replacement for today's
   `unpdf` + hand-rolled `blocks.ts` / `structure.ts` / `docx.ts`. It is
   licence-compatible in the way OpenResume (AGPL-3.0) was not, which was the
   blocker the July 2026 survey hit. `firecrawl/pdf-inspector`'s classifier —
   TextBased / Scanned / ImageBased / Mixed — is worth taking at import time, to
   answer "is this PDF even text" before anything downstream tries.
   *Why this is now in scope:* under rewrite-based tailoring the model papered
   over bad extraction. Under selection, hunt can only choose among lines it
   actually read, so anything import loses does not exist downstream.
   **`pdf-inspector` must never be wired into `parse-fidelity.ts`.** It is
   position-aware and recovers tables and links — far smarter than a real ATS.
   Using it there would raise the fidelity count while employers' parsers stayed
   as naive as ever, which is the invented number arriving by the back door. The
   argument is the one `parser-adapter.ts` already makes about re-parsing with an
   LLM, and it applies unchanged.
3. **Selection-first tailoring.** The run proposes include / drop / reorder over
   the author's own lines, each justified against a line of the posting. Rewrite
   becomes an explicit per-line request. A run that finds nothing worth changing
   says so and recommends the base version.
4. **Counts, always on.** Parse fidelity and keyword coverage run on render and
   display beside the page count. Dropped fields named by path and clickable.
5. **Real links.** `\href` in all three templates; verify parse fidelity stops
   reporting `projects[].url` as dropped.
   *Implementation trap:* `latex.ts` escapes `%` and `#`, and both are legal and
   common in URLs. A URL run through `tex()` and dropped into `\href{}` will
   break. The URL argument needs its own escaping rule, separate from the text
   one, and it needs a test with a query string in it.
6. **One surface.** Collapse dashboard / board / table; outreach and sourcing
   marked "coming soon" in nav.
   *Undecided, and it blocks the step:* which one survives. `/pipeline` already
   holds both board and table behind a toggle, so the cheap answer is that it
   survives and `/` redirects to it — but the dashboard also carries stats and
   "follow-ups due today", and follow-ups are outreach, which is leaving. Decide
   what, if anything, of the dashboard is worth keeping before writing code.
7. **Run the gate.** Apply to one real job, end to end, no code changes — and
   send hunt's résumé rather than the uploaded one.
8. Then re-open the rest of C with what that run taught.

Out of scope for A′, tracked: emphasis vocabulary, voice enforcement,
discovery-as-feed, interview prep, per-JD progress notes.

## What I noticed about how you think

- You debug products the way you'd debug code. You opened with *"idk what it is
  about it"* and then produced five specifics — links, leaving things out,
  formatting, extraction, bold — that turned out to be one design fact. You
  found the root cause by accumulating symptoms rather than theorising, which is
  exactly what you did to me when you asked *"did you really dogfood this?"*
- You picked the harder, more useful thing over the popular one, and justified it
  on utility rather than principle: *"most people prefer a score but for me and
  my goals it's not about that so I can focus on what's real."* The repo had
  already reached that conclusion and defended it worse.
- You reach for leverage before effort — *"this can be done by forking one of the
  competitor open source projects"* — which is the right instinct, applied to the
  wrong half. What you'd have forked is the commodity you already own.
- When you disagree you go quiet rather than argue. *"i suppose"* was the only
  signal that my three-counts screen hadn't fully landed, and it took a follow-up
  to find out it had. Worth knowing about yourself when the next person proposes
  something to you.
